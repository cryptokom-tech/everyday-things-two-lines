# Candidate entries — Everyday Things, Two Lines, Longer Run

Gate: whole entry 1–400 characters. Line 1 = one vivid sentence, analogy,
explains the thing to a 10-year-old. Line 2 = one sentence, what the
analogy maps + what it leaves out. All five verified within the gate.

## A — Toaster / sunbed (SUBMITTED, 323 chars)

> A toaster is a tiny sunbed for bread: it lies flat under hot glowing wires until its top turns golden brown, then pops up when it's done.
> The sunbed maps the heat, the lying flat and the golden-brown tan, but leaves out that bread can't get up and walk away — and a toaster never asks how dark you want it, it just guesses.

Strongest: mapping is accurate (heat, flat, browning, pop-up); analogy is
concrete and surprising (a 10-year-old can picture a sunbed); line 2 names
three mappings and two specific omissions.

## B — Traffic jam / bathtub (336 chars)

> A traffic jam is a bathtub full of toy cars with the plug still in: every car wants to get out through the same tiny drain, so they all just bob around and wait.
> The bathtub maps the too-many-cars and the one-small-exit, but leaves out that real drivers get angry and honk, and that nobody can pull the plug to let them all out at once.

## C — Rain / shower (373 chars)

> Rain is the sky taking a shower with a watering can that has a million tiny holes: the clouds tip over and water falls on everyone below, whether they want a wash or not.
> The shower maps the falling water and the clouds tipping over, but leaves out that rain is cold, that nobody turned the tap on, and that the sky never runs out of hot water — it just runs out of clouds.

## D — Queue / caterpillar (369 chars)

> A queue is a sleepy caterpillar made of people: everyone holds onto the person in front and shuffles forward one tiny step at a time until the head reaches the food.
> The caterpillar maps the single-file shuffling and the slow steps, but leaves out that people in a queue can talk, complain and cut in line — and a real caterpillar never argues about who was here first.

## E — Traffic jam / ketchup bottle (306 chars)

> A traffic jam is a ketchup bottle held upside down: everyone squeezes, nothing comes out for ages, then suddenly it all rushes out at once.
> The bottle maps the waiting and the sudden rush, but leaves out that cars can't be shaken, that the road has no lid to take off, and that the jam comes back tomorrow.

Caveat: the "sudden rush" is not quite how jams clear (they clear
gradually) — if E were chosen, line 2 would need to admit that.
